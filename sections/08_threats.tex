\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{Construct Validity}

Method reachability evaluates a specific part of program understanding. The returned trace is generated by the model and does not directly represent its internal reasoning. Positive traces can stop at the target, while negative traces must cover the complete executable region. Their correctness scores therefore correspond to different exploration requirements. Guard diagnostics identify only errors that can be attributed unambiguously to a false guard. The absence of such an error does not prove that the condition was evaluated correctly. Origin-distance comparisons also exclude literal conditions, unresolved origins, and ambiguous attributions.

\subsection{Internal Validity}

Primary scores are checked against executable-graph evaluations, and prompt comparisons use questions shared by both variants. The independent execution audit in Section~\ref{subsec:oracle-validation} also checks the oracle edges, reachable regions, and labels for the complete final dataset, with each class started through its root launcher. This validation concerns those concrete execution contexts, but does not validate model-output parsing or every diagnostic. Structural factors also change text length and graph properties, so the average score differences do not isolate semantic causes.

In \benchmark~(CO), the entry context and determinism remain implicit. Some errors may therefore come from how the model interprets the source method's arguments. \benchmark~(EE) supplies the entry point and clarifies determinism together, which means that their individual contributions cannot be separated. The Gemma and Qwen3.6 comparison also changes the reasoning budget. Qwen3.8 uses the same requested budget in both variants, but is evaluated on only six difficult classes. Gemma's provisional comparison uses an unbalanced subset and must be updated before drawing conclusions about the full corpus. Decoding, quantization, and parsing can affect all scores, and the serving logs do not identify fixed checkpoint or tokenizer revisions.

\subsection{External and Conclusion Validity}

The generated programs contain one class with controlled method calls and value flow. They do not cover dynamic dispatch, executed recursion, exceptions, external libraries, or dependencies across classes. Without access to training data, generating new programs does not establish the absence of overlap. The findings therefore describe the evaluated tasks and configurations, rather than general repository-level capability.

Each question receives one response, and questions from the same class are related observations. We report descriptive comparisons, without significance tests or confidence intervals for a broader population. Relationships between guard errors and answers are observed in the returned outputs and do not establish causal mechanisms. The fixed-budget prompt subset also does not represent the full factorial grid.
