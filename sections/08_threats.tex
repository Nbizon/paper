\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Construct Validity}

\benchmark evaluates a Boolean reachability decision and an exploration trace against an executable graph. The trace is generated output, rather than a direct observation of internal reasoning. A correct answer with an incorrect trace can reflect several processes, including an incorrect explanation produced after a correct decision. The diagnostics cannot distinguish them.

Guard attribution also has limits. A method-only trace does not identify which guard was taken when several callers could explain an unsupported introduction. We exclude ambiguous attributions, but this may preferentially retain simpler failures. Exposure counts guards in listed methods, not internally evaluated conditions. D5 means no attributed error among exposed guards; it is not a proof of correct evaluation. Origin-distance diagnostics exclude literals and unresolved lineages, and their guard-weighted means differ from D9's question-weighted mean.

\subsection{Internal Validity}

The score depends on generation, the oracle, prompting, inference, parsing, and evaluation. We checked that both models received the same questions and that primary dashboard scores agree with the corrected executable-graph CSVs in every joint stratum. This verifies the aggregation but is not an independent proof of the complete generator and oracle. An error in executable metadata would affect both models' trace scores.

The factorial grid balances the remaining configured factors in marginal comparisons. However, conditions, parameters, and class size also change text length and graph properties. Their contrasts do not isolate a causal semantic effect. Similarly, target-directed guard errors are computed from the same response as the answer: their association with false positives is diagnostic, not evidence that the trace caused the decision. A controlled intervention on value-origin distance, disabled-call placement, and prompt length is needed to test these mechanisms.

\subsection{External and Conclusion Validity}

The generated single-class Java programs omit many properties of real software, including dynamic dispatch, inheritance, recursion, exceptions, external libraries, and cross-class dependencies. Their generated conditions use limited value flow. The observations therefore apply to controlled executable reachability and do not directly establish performance on debugging, repair, or code review.

The study covers two checkpoints and one requested reasoning budget, with one response per question. Multiple queries share a Java class and are not independent replications. We report descriptive scores rather than significance tests or confidence intervals for a larger model population. The family comparison concerns the zero-guard and guarded strata of this grid; it is not a paired intervention on identical programs.

Published external comparisons use different prompts, budgets, scaffolds, and task variants. Their ranking concordance is descriptive and does not establish correlation or predictive validity. Named checkpoint identifiers also do not guarantee immutable weights: the serving logs leave model and tokenizer revisions unset. The archived request outputs and source hashes support reconstruction of the reported aggregates, but do not resolve every deployment-level reproducibility parameter.
