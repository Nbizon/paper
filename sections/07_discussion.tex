\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:error-analysis}

\subsection{Evaluate Decisions Together with Evidence}

The answer--trace gap shows what a Boolean score leaves unresolved. A correct decision can accompany an exploration that omits executable methods or follows disabled calls. The two scores should therefore be reported together, with polarity-specific coverage requirements. In particular, higher negative answer accuracy in larger classes does not establish better exploration: missing calls can also prevent an incorrect branch from reaching the target.

\subsection{Focus Diagnosis on Executability and Value Flow}

The central diagnostics concern omitted executable methods, inclusions outside the executable region, and the origin distance of values used in guards. They distinguish failures to reconstruct executable behavior from simple name errors. Unknown methods occur in fewer than 1\% of condition-aware responses for both models; they account for much less of the observed difficulty than incorrect use of existing calls.

Following a visible call behind a false guard is consistent with syntactic-call bias. However, a trace is an output, and guards without an attributed error are not evidence of correct internal evaluation. The observed patterns motivate controlled experiments that vary value-origin distance or disabled-branch placement while holding graph size and text length comparable. They do not identify an internal reasoning procedure.

\subsection{Use the Artifact for Detailed Error Inspection}

Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic-definitions} retains the error taxonomy, diagnostic codes, populations, and exclusions. The dashboard groups archived errors by model configuration, structural factors, and local graph motif. It supports inspection of examples behind the measured patterns; benchmark scores come from the archived evaluation records. Additional diagnostic categories remain useful for auditing responses without requiring readers to track their codes throughout the main argument.
