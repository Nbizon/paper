\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:error-analysis}

\subsection{Execution Context and Model Performance}

Making the execution context explicit gives only modest average improvements in trace correctness for Gemma and Qwen3.6. The models still encounter difficulties when they must follow values through guarded calls. The difference between answer and trace correctness therefore remains even when the execution information is available.

The same 216 difficult questions make this difference between models more visible. Gemma's trace correctness changes from 26.39\% to 27.31\%, while Qwen3.6 remains at 29.17\%, although its answer correctness increases from 62.04\% to 72.22\%. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next instead gains 11.11 points in trace correctness, from 35.65\% to 46.76\%, including a gain of 17.59 points on negative traces. The improvement therefore concerns the returned exploration more strongly for Qwen3.8. This suggests that some models can make better use of explicit execution information when conditions depend on passed values. However, these observations do not isolate the effect of model size or architecture: the Gemma and Qwen3.6 budgets differ between variants, and the subset contains only six classes. Qwen3.8 also produces more non-responses under EE.

\subsection{Reasoning Budgets and Token Cost}

Providing a larger reasoning budget does not mean that every response uses it completely. On the common questions, the mean reasoning length increases from 5,569 to 5,832 tokens for Qwen3.6 and from 7,788 to 9,241 for Gemma. Under EE, 3.22\% of Qwen3.6 responses and 15.08\% of Gemma responses exceed 16,384 reasoning tokens. These observations support the decision to provide more room than in CO. The increase concerns part of the responses, rather than a doubling of average consumption. However, the lengths observed with these budgets do not establish the minimum budget needed for either variant.

The overall token cost remains high. EE prompts contain 36,600 tokens on average for Qwen3.6 and 38,254 for Gemma, reaching approximately 131,000 and 134,000 tokens. Adding the launcher and execution instructions introduces only about 108 extra input tokens on average, so most input tokens come from the class itself. On the difficult subset, Gemma's mean reasoning length increases from 9,067 to 12,709 tokens for a trace gain below one point. More generated reasoning therefore does not necessarily mean that the model provides substantially better executable evidence. Reporting token consumption together with correctness makes it possible to examine both the scores obtained and the resources used to obtain them.
