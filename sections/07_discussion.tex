\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:error-analysis}

\subsection{Implications for Program Understanding}

A correct reachability decision can hide an exploration that follows a disabled call or omits an executable method. For debugging and change-impact inspection, these errors have different possible consequences: an omission can hide behavior relevant to the investigation, while an unsupported call can direct attention to behavior that does not execute in the specified context. A final Boolean score alone does not distinguish them.

The benchmark therefore supports evaluating the method relationships in a model's explanation, including the conditions and passed values on which they depend. Positive questions test whether the evidence reaches the target; negative questions additionally test coverage of the executable region. These are useful criteria when comparing models or prompts for program-understanding assistance. Their practical effect on developer tasks remains to be evaluated on real programs, where dispatch, exceptions, and dependencies across classes introduce additional difficulties.

\subsection{Execution Context and Model Performance}

The modest average trace gains of Gemma and Qwen3.6 under EE suggest that supplying the execution context does not remove the difficulty of following guarded value propagation. The remaining answer--trace gap concerns the reconstruction of executable behavior even with this information available.

The same 216 difficult questions make the contrast clearer. Gemma's trace correctness changes from 26.39\% to 27.31\%, and Qwen3.6 remains at 29.17\%, although its answer correctness increases from 62.04\% to 72.22\%. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next gains 11.11 points in trace correctness, from 35.65\% to 46.76\%, including a gain of 17.59 points on negative traces. The improvement thus concerns the supporting exploration more strongly for Qwen3.8. It suggests that some models can use the explicit execution context more effectively when guards and passed values interact. These observations do not isolate model size or architecture: the Gemma and Qwen3.6 budgets differ between variants, and the subset contains only six classes. Qwen3.8 also produces more non-responses under EE.

\subsection{Reasoning Budgets and Token Cost}

A larger available budget does not imply that every response consumes it. On the common questions, mean reasoning length increases from 5,569 to 5,832 tokens for Qwen3.6 and from 7,788 to 9,241 for Gemma. Under EE, 3.22\% of Qwen3.6 responses and 15.08\% of Gemma responses exceed 16,384 reasoning tokens, supporting the decision to provide more room than in CO. The increase is concentrated in part of the response population, rather than a doubling of average consumption. These are observed lengths under the chosen budgets; they do not establish the minimum budget needed for either variant.

The overall token cost is nevertheless high. EE prompts average 36,600 tokens for Qwen3.6 and 38,254 for Gemma, reaching approximately 131,000 and 134,000 tokens. Adding the launcher and execution instructions costs only about 108 extra input tokens on average; most input tokens come from the supplied class. On the difficult subset, Gemma's mean reasoning length rises from 9,067 to 12,709 tokens for a trace gain below one point. More generated reasoning therefore does not necessarily yield substantially better executable evidence. Reporting prompt length and reasoning consumption alongside correctness helps distinguish an improvement in accuracy from an improvement in evaluation efficiency.
