\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\benchmark provides a controlled setting for studying whether LLMs can connect executable method calls. Generated Java programs come with reachability labels and an executable-graph oracle, allowing the final decision and its supporting trace to be evaluated independently. Unguarded programs contain no generated conditions; condition-aware programs require the model to distinguish visible calls from calls that can execute.

The two-model study shows a substantial difference between producing the correct answer and providing valid executable evidence. Qwen obtains the strongest overall scores, but its advantage narrows when conditions depend on passed values. Both models' attributed guard errors involve longer value-origin dependencies, and their negative traces often both omit executable methods and enter non-executable regions. Larger classes change how often those erroneous branches lead toward the target: negative answer accuracy can improve while the supporting exploration becomes less reliable.

These observations motivate evaluating program understanding through both decisions and checkable evidence. They also identify follow-up experiments: vary value-origin distance while holding text length and graph structure fixed, and vary the placement of disabled target-reaching calls without changing guard difficulty. Such interventions are needed to test the explanations suggested by the traces. The present findings characterize a controlled reachability task for two checkpoints; they do not establish causal mechanisms or general performance on repository-level development.
