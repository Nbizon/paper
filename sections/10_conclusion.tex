\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents \benchmark, a controlled benchmark for studying method reachability in generated Java programs. Each question comes with a Boolean label and an executable-graph oracle, allowing the final decision and its supporting trace to be evaluated independently. Unguarded questions contain no generated conditions; condition-aware questions require the model to determine which visible calls can execute from the values used in their guards.

\vspace{0.2cm}

The evaluation of Gemma~4 and Qwen~3.6 on 46,656 shared questions shows that correct decisions often lack valid executable evidence. Qwen obtains the strongest overall scores, but its advantage narrows when conditions depend on passed values. Both models' attributed guard errors are concentrated on longer value-origin dependencies. Negative traces also frequently omit executable methods or include methods outside the executable region. These difficulties are obscured by answer accuracy alone: larger classes can improve negative decisions while the corresponding traces become less complete and less accurate.

\vspace{0.2cm}

The main contribution is to evaluate decisions together with checkable evidence and locate failures around executability, exhaustive coverage, and value flow. These output-based patterns motivate further tests rather than establish causal explanations. Independent compilation and instrumented execution checks of the oracle, followed by a paired sensitivity study with explicit source invocations, are needed to strengthen their interpretation. Extending the evaluation to more models and real programs would then test how broadly the observed difficulties transfer.
