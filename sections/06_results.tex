\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the results for condition-aware reachability (generator mode \texttt{d}). Each model is evaluated on the same 46,656 questions, and responses that cannot be parsed remain in all denominators. The \texttt{n\_if}$=0$ stratum is the zero-guard level of this condition-aware experiment; it is not an unguarded/control-flow dataset.

\vspace{0.2cm}

Before computing the scores, we compared every label with the reachability obtained from the executable graph. No disagreement was found. We also applied the earlier reconstruction based on visible calls. For 13,273 of the 23,328 negative questions, this reconstruction incorrectly made the target appear reachable. This difference explains why the previous evaluation imposed an artificial limit on negative path accuracy.

\subsection{\researchq{1}: Reachability Accuracy and Structural Factors}

Table~\ref{tab:overall} reports the two primary metrics. Qwen obtains 87.98\% final-answer correctness, compared with 84.21\% for Gemma. The difference is larger for the returned paths: Qwen reaches 67.43\%, while Gemma reaches 55.90\%. Responses that cannot be parsed are also more frequent for Gemma, at 2.24\%, than for Qwen, at 0.83\%.

\input{tables/overall\_performance}

Distance has a stronger effect on the paths than on the final answers. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:distance}, increasing the distance from 1 to 25 reduces Gemma's final-answer score by 12.54 percentage points and its path score by 36.50 points. For Qwen, the corresponding reductions are 8.20 and 25.95 points.

\vspace{0.2cm}

At distance 25, both models still answer at least 80\% of the questions correctly. Their path scores are lower, reaching 43.58\% for Gemma and 57.23\% for Qwen. This shows that a model can preserve the correct reachability decision even when the sequence used to support this decision becomes incomplete or invalid.

\input{tables/distance\_performance}

Table~\ref{tab:factors} compares the lowest and highest values of each generated factor. Guards produce the largest change in path correctness. Moving from no guard to five guards reduces this score by 44.78 points for Gemma and 47.73 points for Qwen. Parameters have the next largest association, with reductions of 23.83 and 38.50 points. Comments, local variables, and loops are associated with smaller changes.

\vspace{0.2cm}

Class size affects the two models differently. Between 100 and 500 methods, Gemma's final-answer score decreases from 86.81\% to 80.67\%, and its path score decreases from 64.83\% to 45.45\%. Qwen's final-answer score changes little over the same range, while its path score decreases from 69.55\% to 64.93\%.

\input{tables/factor\_effects}

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{1}.}
Both models answer most condition-aware reachability questions correctly, with Qwen obtaining the highest score. The returned paths are less reliable and are more strongly affected by distance, guards, parameters, and class size.

\subsection{\researchq{2}: Evidence from Generated Paths}

Table~\ref{tab:trace-diagnostics} separates reachable and unreachable questions. For reachable targets, Qwen obtains 94.08\% final-answer correctness and 85.72\% path correctness. Gemma obtains 85.19\% and 73.96\%, respectively.

\vspace{0.2cm}

The difference is larger for unreachable targets. Path correctness falls to 49.15\% for Qwen and 37.84\% for Gemma. A reachable question only requires the path to end when it reaches the target. An unreachable question requires the model to cover the complete set of methods reachable from the source. It therefore asks for a more exhaustive trace.

\input{tables/trace\_diagnostics}

The executable-graph evaluation removes the former artificial ceiling because it ignores calls that are visible in the source code but cannot execute. It also accepts different exploration orders when they follow the same executable graph. Path correctness therefore measures whether the returned sequence provides valid executable evidence, rather than whether it reproduces an order selected during generation.

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{2}.}
The paths reveal a clear difference between producing the correct answer and providing a valid sequence of calls. This difference is especially visible for unreachable targets, for which the trace must be exhaustive.

\subsection{\researchq{3}: Relation to Existing Benchmarks}

The current experiment contains two model configurations evaluated on the condition-aware task. It does not contain results for these exact configurations on an external code-reasoning benchmark. Moreover, two models are not sufficient to study a meaningful relationship between benchmark scores.

\vspace{0.2cm}

We therefore report no correlation and do not assume that the ranking observed on \benchmark also applies to existing benchmarks. A broader comparison would require more models evaluated under matched configurations.

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{3}.}
The available results describe performance on controlled method reachability. They do not establish a relationship with performance on external benchmarks.
