\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We first examine whether the models answer reachability questions correctly and whether their traces follow executable calls. We then compare the two prompting variants of the same benchmark. The analyses of structural factors and traces use the 46,656 questions evaluated with \benchmark~(CO), while the prompt comparisons use questions shared by both variants within each model.

\subsection{\researchq{1}: Reachability Accuracy and Structural Factors}

\subsubsection{Correct Decisions Do Not Always Have Valid Evidence}

Table~\ref{tab:overall} shows that Qwen obtains the highest overall scores, with 87.98\% final-answer correctness and 67.43\% trace correctness, compared with 84.21\% and 55.90\% for Gemma. The difference between the models is therefore larger for the trace than for the final answer. However, a correct answer is accompanied by an incorrect trace in 28.31\% of Gemma responses and 20.55\% of Qwen responses. This shows that answer correctness alone does not indicate whether the model provides a valid exploration.

\input{tables/overall_performance}

Without guards, Qwen reaches 99.19\% answer correctness and 96.33\% trace correctness, while Gemma reaches 96.05\% and 82.23\%. When conditions are present, trace correctness falls to 57.80\% and 47.13\%, respectively. Non-responses represent only 1.10\% and 2.73\% of condition-aware questions. They therefore explain much less of the difficulty than the difference between answer and trace correctness. The main problem concerns whether the model can provide valid executable evidence.

\subsubsection{Conditions and Parameters Act Together}

Increasing \mbox{\texttt{n\_if}} from one to five reduces condition-aware trace correctness by 21.94 points for Gemma and 19.17 for Qwen (Table~\ref{tab:factors}). The largest difference associated with \path{n_params} appears when the first parameter is introduced. Gemma's trace score falls from 66.62\% to 39.17\%, and Qwen's from 90.53\% to 43.52\%.

\input{tables/factor_effects}

Considering \mbox{\texttt{n\_if}} and \path{n_params} together makes it possible to locate this difficulty more precisely. With \mbox{\texttt{n\_if}$=0$}, increasing \path{n_params} from zero to two reduces trace correctness by only 2.26 points for Gemma and 0.44 for Qwen. With \mbox{\texttt{n\_if}$=5$}, these reductions reach 33.82 and 55.66 points. This pattern shows that the effect of \path{n_params} is greater when conditions use passed values to determine which calls execute. \path{n_comment}, \path{n_vars}, and \path{n_loops} show smaller average differences in this grid, with none exceeding five points for either model.

\subsubsection{Distance and Class Size}

For positive condition-aware questions, increasing the nominal distance in \path{depths} from 1 to 25 reduces trace correctness from 95.68\% to 56.58\% for Gemma and from 99.90\% to 70.52\% for Qwen. For negative questions, it falls from 54.01\% to 11.63\% and from 55.56\% to 22.94\%. At magnitude 25, the shortest executable route for a positive question contains 5.90 methods on average, while a negative trace must cover a region of 26 methods. Since positive distance records the target's position in the reference exploration, a valid trace can omit earlier side branches. The same nominal distance therefore does not require the same amount of exploration for both polarities.

When \path{class_size} increases from 100 to 500 methods, condition-aware trace correctness decreases from 55.70\% to 37.23\% for Gemma and from 60.11\% to 54.99\% for Qwen. However, Qwen's answer score increases from 82.46\% to 85.67\%. Its positive answer correctness falls from 97.67\% to 86.68\%, while its negative correctness rises from 67.25\% to 84.67\%. A higher average answer score can therefore coexist with less reliable exploration in larger classes.

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{1}.}
Qwen obtains the best overall scores, but both models are less reliable at producing a valid trace than at answering the question. The main difficulties involve guarded calls, parameters used in conditions, and the complete exploration required by negative questions.

\subsection{\researchq{2}: What the Traces Reveal}

\subsubsection{Executability and Incomplete Exploration}

On condition-aware questions, Gemma obtains 69.27\% trace correctness for positive targets but only 24.99\% for negative targets. Qwen obtains 81.57\% and 34.04\%, respectively. For negative questions, 51.38\% of Gemma traces and 48.50\% of Qwen traces include an existing method outside the executable region. They also omit a large part of this region, with a mean omitted fraction of 39.37\% and 38.01\%. A trace can contain both types of errors, so these values should not be added as separate categories.

A model can follow a call behind a false guard without necessarily changing its final answer. The consequence depends on whether this disabled branch leads toward the target. Among eligible negative responses with an attributed guard error whose callee can reach the target, the answer is a false positive in 89.73\% of Gemma cases and 92.57\% of Qwen cases. Without such an attributed error, these rates are 1.36\% and 0.46\%. This allows us to relate errors in the trace to errors in the answer, without establishing that one caused the other.

\subsubsection{Value Propagation Across Methods}
\label{subsec:value-origin}

With \path{n_params}=0, false-guard errors are attributed to only 0.25\% of Gemma's eligible guard exposures and 0.18\% of Qwen's. With \path{n_params}=1, these ratios increase to 7.42\% and 9.01\%, and with \path{n_params}=2 they reach 10.40\% and 11.11\%.

The distance between a guard and the declaration supplying its value also helps characterize these errors. At \path{n_params}=1, guards with an attributed error have a mean origin distance of 5.41 method boundaries for Gemma, compared with 3.59 for exposed guards without an attributed error. Qwen shows a similar difference, with 5.15 versus 3.58. At \path{n_params}=2, these values become 6.22 versus 4.47 for Gemma and 5.90 versus 4.47 for Qwen. When considering only the common population with unambiguous attribution, the mean distance of exposed guards is almost identical between models: 3.72 at \path{n_params}=1 and approximately 4.64 at \path{n_params}=2.

These averages are weighted by guard occurrences and exclude literal conditions, unresolved origins, and ambiguous attributions. They show that attributed errors involve longer value dependencies, but do not estimate how failure probability changes with each additional method boundary. Qwen's lead in condition-aware trace correctness also decreases from 23.91 points at \path{n_params}=0 to 4.35 at \path{n_params}=1 and 3.75 at \path{n_params}=2. Its higher overall score therefore does not remove the difficulty both models encounter when values are passed between methods.

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{2}.}
The traces show calls without executable support, methods omitted from the reachable region, and errors involving longer value dependencies. This makes it possible to identify difficulties that would not be visible from answer correctness alone.

\subsection{\researchq{3}: Explicit Execution Context and Determinism}

\subsubsection{Comparison on the Main Evaluation Set}

Table~\ref{tab:prompt-comparison} compares \benchmark~(CO) and \benchmark~(EE) on the same questions and oracles. For Qwen, answer correctness increases from 87.98\% to 89.66\%, while trace correctness increases from 67.43\% to 69.23\%. These gains of 1.68 and 1.79 points remain small compared with the difference between answer and trace correctness. On condition-aware questions, negative answer correctness rises from 75.85\% to 80.16\%, while positive correctness remains around 92.6\%.

\input{tables/prompt_comparison}

Gemma shows the same overall trend on the common questions. Its answer correctness increases from 74.85\% to 76.87\%, and its trace correctness from 43.12\% to 44.72\%. Non-responses decrease for both models. The Gemma values in Table~\ref{tab:prompt-comparison} are provisional and use the same 11,824 questions in both variants. They should therefore not be compared directly with its full-grid averages in Table~\ref{tab:overall}.

The difficulty with passed values remains visible in Qwen's complete comparison. Under \benchmark~(EE), condition-aware trace correctness is 92.06\% at \path{n_params}=0, 46.43\% at \path{n_params}=1, and 41.66\% at \path{n_params}=2. Making the execution context explicit is therefore associated with an improvement, but does not remove the large difference involving \path{n_params}. The EE configuration also provides a larger reasoning budget to accommodate execution analysis. These scores describe the results under the chosen budgets, while Section~\ref{sec:error-analysis} discusses token consumption.

\subsubsection{Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at a Fixed Budget}

On the 216-question difficult subset, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next improves from 57.41\% to 63.89\% answer correctness and from 35.65\% to 46.76\% trace correctness between \benchmark~(CO) and \benchmark~(EE). Both variants use a reasoning budget of 16,384 tokens. The largest improvements concern negative questions, where answer correctness rises from 24.07\% to 46.30\% and trace correctness from 0\% to 17.59\%. For positive questions, trace correctness increases from 71.30\% to 75.93\%, while answer correctness decreases from 90.74\% to 81.48\%.

However, non-responses also increase from 23 to 42 out of 216 questions, or from 10.65\% to 19.44\%. The higher overall scores are therefore accompanied by fewer responses with a recoverable answer. Non-responses remain in the score denominators. These results concern a specific subset with guarded calls and parameters, so they do not establish Qwen3.8's ranking on the full benchmark.

\paragraph{Answer to \researchq{3}.}
The same dataset gives higher overall answer and trace scores with \benchmark~(EE), particularly for negative questions. This is consistent with the additional difficulty of inferring the execution assumptions in \benchmark~(CO). However, making these assumptions explicit does not remove difficulties with guarded value propagation. The main comparison also changes the reasoning budget, while the fixed-budget Qwen3.8 comparison shows both improved traces and more non-responses.
